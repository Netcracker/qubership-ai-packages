## Summary for the PR author

I rewrote the 20 tests the commit added, following `test-authoring` §7's rule on report quality: before touching anything, I broke two cases on purpose in a scratch test and confirmed empirically that `CompilationTestHelper.doTest()` (JUnit 4 engine, no assertion library of its own — §0 rule 5) **stops at the first mismatch and names it by line number**, not by content, and does not report every mismatch in one compilation. That's the exact mechanism the skill's own worked example ("A case and its control in one act") describes, so its rule applied directly: a silent (non-reporting) case needs a reporting control to prove its silence isn't just a broken setup, and the two may share one compiled source only when each input's outcome holds independently of the others; two *different* reporting cases must stay in separate tests, since a regression in the first would mask the second.

Working from the production diff (`CheckIdenticalNullabilityVisitor`/`GenericsUtils`), I traced each of the 20 added tests to the exact branch it exercises and merged the ones that differ in exactly one respect and share identical class-level declarations into three decision-table tests, cutting 20 tests to 13 (−114 lines) with no coverage lost:
